# Polyandry-Database

## Project Introduction

**Why did male-only parental care (male uniparental care) evolve in systems where females have multiple social mates (social polyandry)? What factors influence the evolutionary costs and benefits for these parenting males?**

This is a database of information about a variety of socially polyandrous species, which I will be applying to a mathematical model of the role of extrapair paternity in the evolution of male uniparental care in these systems.


## Data Dictionary (metadata)
* ‘Species’: Scientific name of the species studied in the source paper
* ‘Authors’: Author(s) of the source paper
* ‘Title’: Title of the source paper
* ‘Publication Year’: Year that the source paper was published
* ‘Link’: Link to the source paper for ease of reference
* ‘Overall rate of EPP (%)’: Mean rate of extrapair paternity for the given species, as found in the source paper 
* ‘Parental care description’: Brief summary of the focal species’ parental care system in case of nuances in categories
* ‘Parental care category’: Categorical description of parental care in the focal species as one of the following: ‘male-only’, ‘primarily male’, ‘biparental’, ‘primarily female’, ‘female only’
* ‘Average clutch size’: Mean number of eggs in a nest for the focal species
* ‘Incubation time (days)’: Mean duration (in days) of incubation of focal species after clutch completion
* ‘Parenting time (days)’: Mean duration (in days) of parental care for chicks of focal species after hatching
* ‘Notes’: Additional notes about the source paper or the focal species that may be relevant for usage of these data
